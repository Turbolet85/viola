## Relevant amendment history

- **2026-10-04-dialog-answers-by-dialog-id** — wrote the §8 Timeout extensions (CLI) clause this chunk moves. The old "viola imposes no limit on a human" became: a raised dialog takes a `viola answer` only within `DIALOG_DEADLINE` (60 s PROVISIONAL, under the `hooks.json` `timeout` of 75 s); at expiry the hook exits 0 with no body and the dialog renders for the human in the `claude` TUI; a later `answer` is refused `not-delivered` / `unknown-dialog` (exit 13). Its stated reason is that the limit falls on the driver's answer path and the fail-open contract leaves the dialog to the human. Every value in it (60 s, 75 s, `unknown-dialog`, and the absence of any expiry record) is a value this chunk may change, so the clause is re-read against the landed hold and amended at the wrap. A viola-built deny at expiry would break the "left to the human" reasoning.
- **2026-10-04-the-wheel** — fixed the tui wheel boundary the hand-back relies on. It defined the closed non-editing set (focus reports, mouse reports, terminal replies), and every other byte counts as editing and takes the wheel. Case (3) probes the wheel with an `answer` to no pending dialog (exit 13 for the driver, 10 for the human). On `windows-2025` it records a platform fact: an injected mouse report arrives as win32 key-down records and takes the wheel. This chunk's three-OS outer-PTY hold case runs under the same rules: a human key during the hold is any editing byte, and the Windows leg's asymmetry stands.
- **2026-10-07-send-waits-out-the-paste-hint** — the precedent for a driver-side wait that a human key must win. The §8 clause is worded so that the bound (`GATE_MAX_WAIT`) "falls on the driver only", and a key during the wait goes to the child and takes the wheel at once. The outer-PTY case `send_under_the_paste_hint_a_human_key_during_the_gate_wait_wins` is sequenced on the wrapper's `channel-request` line and `wheel` record, never on a timer, and its limit (it cannot tell one wheel read from the next) is stated, with the unit tier carrying that proof. Both the sequencing and the stated-limit form fit the hold-plus-human-key case and `hand_back` past a long or configured hold.
- **2026-10-08-first-live-test-and-self-drive** — extended the non-editing list with seven exact-grammar `CSI` replies, and recorded the one live takeover reading on Linux: a `wtype` key wrote `wheel{human, human-input}`, and the next driver `send` exited 10. That reading is supplemental and never gating. Any live reading of a long hold handed back by a key takes the same status, and per the handoff's standing rule it reads `hyprctl locked` first.
- **2026-10-10-statusline-pass-through** — gave a start variant its own zero-viola-bytes reading (`passthrough_with_a_statusline_source_adds_no_viola_bytes`). The §8 list names every bound viola holds and whom it falls on, so a longer or configured hold (and any per-start hook `timeout`) gets its sentence in the same form.
- **2026-10-10-budget-governor** — the source of the pattern the extract names. `path6_a_reading_over_the_threshold_refuses_the_send` is a state-specific Tooling reading: a human key reaches the child, and the outer PTY holds none of `unable`, `hint:` or the state's code.
